\# Phase 6 — Evidence Gap Priority Analysis



\## 1. Purpose



This document prioritizes the evidence gaps identified across the 10 TB candidate compounds.



Priority is based on how frequently a gap occurs and how important the information is for evaluating the quality and completeness of the research dataset.



\## 2. Priority Levels



\### High Priority



These gaps occur across many candidates and significantly affect evidence comparison:



\* Quantitative potency data

\* Quantitative selectivity data

\* Assay-specific evidence



\### Medium Priority



These gaps affect the completeness of pharmacokinetic and safety evaluation:



\* Human pharmacokinetic data

\* Detailed ADME information

\* Detailed safety/toxicity evidence



\### Candidate-Specific Priority



Some candidates have additional gaps requiring individual attention.



\* \*\*C-005 Bedaquiline:\*\* human PK and detailed safety extraction

\* \*\*C-007 Pretomanid:\*\* detailed PK and safety extraction

\* \*\*C-009 Clofazimine:\*\* human PK and safety extraction

\* \*\*C-010 Sutezolid:\*\* complete human ADME and mature safety evidence



\## 3. Overall Priority Order



| Priority | Evidence Area            | Reason                                                     |

| -------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |

| 1        | Quantitative potency     | Needed for consistent comparison of antimicrobial activity |

| 2        | Quantitative selectivity | Needed to characterize specificity and off-target evidence |

| 3        | Assay-specific evidence  | Needed to understand the context of reported measurements  |

| 4        | Human PK/ADME            | Improves understanding of pharmacokinetic evidence         |

| 5        | Safety/toxicity          | Improves completeness of candidate safety documentation    |



\## 4. Important Limitation



A missing value in this dataset does not necessarily mean that the information does not exist in scientific literature.



It means that the information was not sufficiently captured or structured in the current research dataset.



\## 5. Recommended Next Research Step



Future evidence collection should focus first on quantitative potency and selectivity information, while recording the experimental or assay context for every value.



After that, human PK/ADME and detailed safety evidence should be strengthened.



\## 6. Conclusion



The evidence-gap analysis provides a clear roadmap for improving the TB candidate-compound dataset.



The highest-priority gaps are quantitative potency, quantitative selectivity, and assay-specific evidence.
