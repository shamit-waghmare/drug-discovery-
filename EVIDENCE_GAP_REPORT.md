\# Phase 6 — Evidence Gap Report



\## 1. Objective



Phase 6 evaluates the completeness and strength of the available evidence for the 10 selected Tuberculosis (TB) candidate compounds.



The analysis is based on the existing candidate compound registry and the evidence-strength assessment completed in Phase 4.



\## 2. Candidates Reviewed



A total of 10 candidates were reviewed:



\* C-001 Rifampicin

\* C-002 Isoniazid

\* C-003 Pyrazinamide

\* C-004 Ethambutol

\* C-005 Bedaquiline

\* C-006 Delamanid

\* C-007 Pretomanid

\* C-008 Linezolid

\* C-009 Clofazimine

\* C-010 Sutezolid



\## 3. Major Evidence Gaps



The most important gaps identified were:



1\. Quantitative potency data

2\. Quantitative selectivity data

3\. Assay-specific evidence

4\. Human pharmacokinetic data

5\. Detailed ADME information

6\. Detailed safety/toxicity evidence



\## 4. Candidate-Level Findings



| Candidate    | Main Gap                                                     | Priority |

| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------- |

| Rifampicin   | Potency and selectivity                                      | High     |

| Isoniazid    | Quantitative selectivity and additional potency measurements | High     |

| Pyrazinamide | Potency, selectivity and assay-specific evidence             | High     |

| Ethambutol   | Potency, selectivity and assay-specific evidence             | High     |

| Bedaquiline  | Potency, selectivity, human PK and safety                    | High     |

| Delamanid    | Potency, selectivity and safety evidence                     | High     |

| Pretomanid   | Potency, selectivity, PK and safety                          | High     |

| Linezolid    | TB-specific potency, selectivity and safety                  | High     |

| Clofazimine  | Potency, selectivity, human PK and safety                    | High     |

| Sutezolid    | Potency, selectivity, human ADME and mature safety evidence  | High     |



\## 5. Evidence Strength Interpretation



The identified gaps should not be interpreted as proof that the information does not exist.



A gap means that the information was not sufficiently captured, structured, or verified in the current project dataset.



The Phase 4 scores measure evidence strength and completeness within this research exercise. They do not represent clinical superiority, treatment recommendation, or guaranteed development success.



\## 6. Highest-Priority Research Areas



The recommended order for future evidence collection is:



1\. Quantitative potency

2\. Quantitative selectivity

3\. Assay-specific evidence

4\. Human PK/ADME

5\. Safety and toxicity evidence



\## 7. Dataset-Level Limitation



The current dataset contains strong basic information on candidate identity, targets, mechanisms, therapeutic area, evidence stage, and selected ADME/safety information.



However, several quantitative fields remain incomplete.



This limits direct numerical comparison between candidates.



\## 8. Recommended Next Work



Future research should:



\* Locate reliable quantitative potency measurements.

\* Record the assay context for each measurement.

\* Collect structured selectivity evidence.

\* Strengthen human PK/ADME documentation.

\* Strengthen safety and toxicity documentation.

\* Record the source and confidence level for newly collected evidence.

\* Update the candidate registry after evidence verification.



\## 9. Conclusion



Phase 6 successfully identified the major evidence gaps within the TB candidate-compound dataset.



The most common gaps are quantitative potency and selectivity information, followed by assay-specific evidence and selected PK/ADME and safety information.



The resulting gap analysis provides a structured roadmap for improving the research dataset in later phases.
